================================================================================================
FraudGraph AI — Results, Discussion, and Limitations (Phase 6, step 6)
================================================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Research question
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Does relational graph structure provide robust additional signal for financial fraud detection
under temporal evaluation, and does that signal support fraud investigation and explanation?

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Results, by sub-question
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RQ1 — Does a graph-based model (GraphSAGE) work at all?
  Yes. At an adequately tuned training budget (100 epochs; 30 was undertrained — validation AP
  was still rising sharply, results/tuning_results.txt), GraphSAGE with the full card+merchant
  graph reaches PR-AUC 0.0549 +/- 0.0067 on Window 0, ~3.6x the no-graph ablation (0.0151) and
  ~4.9x Card-only (0.0113). (Table 1, final_results_tables.txt)

RQ2 — Does training budget matter?
  Yes, substantially. 30 epochs undertrains the model relative to its capacity; increasing to
  100 epochs was necessary before any fair comparison to tuned classical baselines was possible.

RQ3 — Does graph structure matter, and which relationship carries the signal?
  Yes, and specifically the MERCHANT relationship, not the card relationship. Card-only
  (0.0113 +/- 0.0004) is statistically indistinguishable from — if anything slightly below —
  No Graph (0.0151 +/- 0.0003). Merchant-only (0.0858 +/- 0.0098) is the single best-performing
  graph configuration tested, beating even the full card+merchant graph (0.0549 +/- 0.0067).
  This is confirmed in 5/5 seeds on Window 0 (edge_ablation_results.txt) and holds — with the
  same ordering — across all 3 additional temporal windows (Table 2). A follow-up architecture
  experiment (Relation-Aware GNN, separate weight matrices per edge type) was run to test
  whether homogeneous aggregation was "diluting" the merchant signal with card noise; it does
  NOT meaningfully close the gap (0.0575 +/- 0.0053, statistically indistinguishable from
  homogeneous Full Graph at 95% CI) — so the mechanism behind Merchant-only's advantage over
  Full Graph is NOT simply representational dilution, and remains an open question.

RQ4 — Does the advantage survive temporal robustness testing?
  Yes, on point estimates in 4/4 windows for both "Full Graph beats XGBoost" and "Merchant-only
  beats Full Graph." Statistical significance (95% CI, t-distribution) is cleaner for the first
  claim than the second: Merchant-only vs Full Graph is significant in Window 0 (5 seeds) and
  Window 1 (3 seeds), but the CIs overlap in Windows 2-3 due to higher per-seed variance at only
  3 seeds each (statistical_analysis.txt). The direction is consistent 4/4 times; the magnitude
  in Windows 2-3 should be described as "consistent with" rather than "proven by" this seed count.

RQ5 — Is the merchant-only advantage a leakage artifact?
  No. A causal graph variant that blocks test-split transactions from informing their merchant
  hub's embedding (source_mask) scores HIGHER than the standard transductive variant
  (0.1410 +/- 0.0167 vs 0.0858 +/- 0.0098, non-overlapping 95% CI) — the opposite of what
  leakage would predict. Combined with the finding that 48/49 test-period fraud merchants
  already had fraud in train (so the model can learn "this merchant is risky" purely from
  training data), the merchant-only advantage is best explained as legitimate reputation
  learning, not information leaking between same-split transactions.

RQ6 — Does the graph help fraud-ring investigation, not just per-transaction detection?
  Partially, and with an important product-level caveat. Louvain communities over the
  high-risk-transaction card graph do concentrate fraud: the top 10 communities (by fraud
  count) contain 66.2% of all fraud transactions while representing only 27.9% of all
  transactions (2.4x concentration). But the app's actual shipped default sort for this list
  (avg_risk_score) captures almost none of that value — top 5 by avg_risk_score capture only
  0.57% of fraud, because that metric is a per-community AVERAGE and gets diluted by large,
  fraud-dense communities' many low-risk transactions. This is a concrete, actionable product
  finding independent of the research question. Also: the "rings" found are dominated by two
  large, diffuse card populations (52 and 49 cards, 1300+ merchants each) rather than many small
  tight coordinated groups — an honest characterization matters more than an impressive-sounding
  community count.

RQ7 — Can the graph's contribution be explained, not just measured?
  Yes. For 29/30 (97%) sampled high-confidence fraud predictions, GNNExplainer's own top-5 edge
  importances include a specific merchant-node connection — evidence a transaction-only
  (feature-based) model could never produce, since it only ever sees the row in front of it.
  Notably, GNNExplainer surfaced this relational evidence in slightly more cases (29) than the
  existing hand-written NETWORK heuristics did (28/30) — a concrete argument for surfacing the
  model's own explanations in the Investigation UI rather than relying solely on hand-engineered
  connectivity counters.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The bug, and why it matters for how these results should be read
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Partway through this project, an explainability run surfaced structurally-impossible
transaction<->transaction edges, which traced back to a bug present in build_graph since before
this project began: merchant node ids were never shifted by the required offset, so "merchant
edges" never reached real merchant nodes. Every merchant-touching experiment (edge ablation,
temporal-window merchant/full-graph runs, the leakage audit, the relation-aware architecture
test, the production model, and the explainability sample) was re-trained from scratch after the
fix — nothing was patched or extrapolated.

The encouraging finding: every qualitative conclusion survived the fix (merchant edges dominate;
graph structure helps; the advantage is temporally robust; there is no leakage). What changed
were magnitudes (roughly halved to a third of the pre-fix numbers) and one specific mechanistic
claim (the relation-aware "dilution" explanation, which reversed). This is disclosed in full via
results/reproducibility_audit.txt, and is itself worth stating explicitly in any write-up: a
graph-construction bug that silently produced a plausible-looking, reproducible, multi-seed,
temporally-robust result is exactly the kind of failure mode that a single-metric, single-split
evaluation would never catch — it took a qualitative inspection (an explanation's edges not
making structural sense) to find it, not a quantitative one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Limitations
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Single dataset. All results are on one sample (IBM TabFormer, 677,930 transactions) from one
   synthetic data generator. Generalization to other fraud datasets/domains is untested.

2. Small positive counts. Window 0's test split has 354 fraud transactions; PR-AUC and
   Precision@200 are high-variance estimators at this scale — a handful of transactions
   reordering can move PR-AUC by hundredths. Seed-to-seed variance partially captures this, but
   a rigorous treatment would additionally bootstrap-resample test-set predictions directly
   (not done here; flagged as future work in statistical_analysis.txt).

3. Small seed counts (3 for per-window sweeps, 5 for Window 0 sweeps), driven by this project's
   CPU-only, single-machine compute budget (each 100-epoch GraphSAGE training run took
   roughly 3-9 minutes; multi-seed x multi-window sweeps took 40 minutes to several hours). This
   widens confidence intervals enough that 2 of 4 temporal windows do not reach significance for
   the Merchant-only vs Full Graph comparison specifically (though the direction is consistent
   4/4 times).

4. Dataset-specific fraud/merchant structure. This dataset's fraud is extremely
   merchant-concentrated (several individual merchants show 80-90%+ fraud rates among their
   transactions) — a data-generating-process property that may not transfer to real-world fraud,
   where such extreme merchant-level concentration is less common. The magnitude of the
   merchant-only advantage found here should be read as a property of this dataset, not assumed
   to generalize in degree (though the qualitative finding — that entity-reputation edges can
   outweigh raw pairwise-transaction edges — plausibly does generalize).

5. Transductive evaluation setting (with the leakage audit as a mitigation, not an elimination).
   Even the "causal" variant still lets every transaction receive its hub's embedding computed
   from train+val history in a single batched forward pass, rather than simulating truly
   sequential real-time scoring transaction-by-transaction. The leakage audit rules out test-test
   contamination specifically, not every form of evaluation-vs-deployment mismatch.

6. Relation-aware architecture: one variant tested (separate SAGEConv per edge type, summed).
   Other architectures (attention-weighted combination, GAT/RGCN-style relation-specific
   transformations, deeper networks) were not tried and might behave differently — the "dilution
   hypothesis is not supported" finding is specific to the one architecture tested, not a claim
   that no architecture could recover Merchant-only's advantage from the full graph.

7. No multiple-comparisons correction was applied across the many pairwise comparisons run over
   the course of this project (see statistical_analysis.txt item 4).

8. The bug's existence for an unknown period before this project (present in the code as
   inherited) means any prior claims made about this codebase's graph-based results, in any
   context predating this audit, should be treated as unverified until checked against
   reproducibility_audit.txt's canonical file list.
