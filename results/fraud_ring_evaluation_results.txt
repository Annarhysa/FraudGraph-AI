================================================================================================

Method: clusters.py connects cards that share a merchant on a high-risk
transaction (gnn_score_pct >= 75), then runs Louvain community detection on
that card graph. This evaluates whether the resulting communities actually
concentrate fraud, and how much of the dataset's fraud an investigator would
find by triaging only the highest-signal communities.

================================================================================================
Verdict
================================================================================================
The top 10 communities by fraud count contain 66.2% of all fraud
transactions while representing only 27.88% of all transactions —
a 2.4x concentration of fraud relative to transaction volume.

IMPORTANT CAVEAT 1 (product finding, not just research): the app's actual default
UI sort (avg_risk_score, section 2b) performs far worse than ranking by fraud
count — top 5 by avg_risk_score captured only 0.57% of fraud, top 20 only ~16.3%,
vs 59.3% and 67.6% respectively when ranked by fraud count. avg_risk_score is
dominated by small, tight, high-risk-looking-but-actually-clean communities, while
the fraud-dense communities are large and their risk score gets averaged down. If
this evaluation is to translate into product value, the ring list's default sort
should change (e.g. total/max fraud-relevant signal, not a per-community average).

IMPORTANT CAVEAT 2: the two dominant communities (RING0004: 52 cards / 1346 merchants / 21167 txns; RING0005: 49 cards / 1293 merchants / 25419 txns) are large and diffuse,
not tight coordinated rings — most of the top-10 fraud capture comes from these two
giant components rather than many small suspicious groups. The remaining ~45
communities (from rank ~11 onward) add essentially zero additional fraud capture
(67.56% plateaus from rank 28 through rank 56). Read 'fraud-ring detection' here as
'two large risk-adjacent card populations + a long tail of small, mostly clean
communities' rather than many discrete criminal rings — a fair characterization for
a thesis, but don't oversell the granularity of what Louvain found on this graph.

-> Enrichment is present but modest/inconsistent across communities — useful
   as a triage signal but should be presented with the caveat that not every
   detected community is fraud-relevant.
