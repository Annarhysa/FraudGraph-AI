================================================================================================

Method: clusters.py connects cards that share a merchant on a high-risk
transaction (gnn_score_pct >= 75), then runs Louvain community detection on
that card graph. This evaluates whether the resulting communities actually
concentrate fraud, and how much of the dataset's fraud an investigator would
find by triaging only the highest-signal communities.

Dataset: 677930 transactions, 2087 fraud (0.3078% overall fraud rate)
Detected communities: 71 (min 2 cards each)

================================================================================================
Verdict
================================================================================================
The top 10 communities by fraud count contain 60.4% of all fraud
transactions while representing only 21.33% of all transactions —
a 2.8x concentration of fraud relative to transaction volume.

IMPORTANT CAVEAT 1 (product finding, not just research): the app's actual default
UI sort (avg_risk_score, section 2b) performs far worse than ranking by fraud
count — top 5 by avg_risk_score captured 0% of fraud, top 20 only ~2.8%, vs 56.5%
and 64.7% respectively when ranked by fraud count. avg_risk_score is dominated by
small, tight, high-risk-looking-but-actually-clean communities, while the fraud-
dense communities are large and their risk score gets averaged down. If this
evaluation is to translate into product value, the ring list's default sort should
change (e.g. total/max fraud-relevant signal, not a per-community average).

IMPORTANT CAVEAT 2: the two dominant communities (RING0002: 57 cards / 1593 merchants / 25850 txns; RING0009: 48 cards / 1434 merchants / 31427 txns) are large and diffuse,
not tight coordinated rings — most of the top-10 fraud capture comes from these two
giant components rather than many small suspicious groups. The remaining ~65
communities (from rank 15 onward) add essentially zero additional fraud capture
(65.50% plateaus from rank 36 through rank 71). Read 'fraud-ring detection' here as
'two large risk-adjacent card populations + a long tail of small, mostly clean
communities' rather than many discrete criminal rings — a fair characterization for
a thesis, but don't oversell the granularity of what Louvain found on this graph.

-> Enrichment is present but modest/inconsistent across communities — useful
   as a triage signal but should be presented with the caveat that not every
   detected community is fraud-relevant.
